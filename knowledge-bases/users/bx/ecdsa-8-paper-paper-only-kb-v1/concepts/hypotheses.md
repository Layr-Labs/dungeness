# Cross-paper research hypotheses

These hypotheses are derived only from the eight papers.

## H1 — denser reversible transcript blocks

The three-symbol Euclid alphabet may admit larger reversible blocks with lower
average storage than the paper's three-step, five-bit code.

Falsifier: decoder cost or reverse-streaming workspace outweighs the saved
transcript width.

## H2 — integer Dialog

A small invertible integer transition alphabet may represent binary or
quotient Euclid while retaining direct action on arbitrary coefficient values.

Falsifier: sign, quotient, or width information requires at least as much
storage as the compact branch transcript.

## H3 — transcript core plus field-register demolition

Luo's full-register measurement protocol may wrap a transcript-based
multiplier if the deleted value is exactly reconstructible from surviving
operands.

Falsifier: reconstruction requires the same field register or equivalent
workspace that demolition was intended to release.

## H4 — exact liveness without moving-value arithmetic

Luo's active-window proofs may permit physical sharing while retaining
fixed-position arithmetic.

Falsifier: coherent input-dependent boundaries require the literal
location-controlled operations.

## H5 — vented pseudo-Mersenne correction

Gidney's carry venting may reduce workspace for additions of the short
pseudo-Mersenne correction constant.

Falsifier: the prime-field comparison and canonicalization shell dominates the
complete primitive.

## H6 — caller-aware fresh-output multiplication

Litinski's fresh product may become useful when its sole consumer can absorb
the product before reverse cleanup, following Häner's producer-consumer
pattern.

Falsifier: the copied result or Montgomery representation remains live across
the same interval as the inputs.
