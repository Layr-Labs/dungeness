# Source: IACR ePrint 2020/077

## One-paragraph synthesis

**[PAPER]** Häner et al. provide a complete, testable Q# implementation of ECDLP arithmetic, improving the then-standard RNSL circuit through windowed Montgomery arithmetic, adaptive uncomputation/pebbling, a swap-normalized Kaliski inversion round, pseudo-inverse correction during uncomputation, and aggressive workspace reuse. It is the mature integrated baseline in this KB.

## Point-addition shell

The affine Weierstrass point-add circuit is summarized as:

```text
2 modular divisions
2 modular multiplications
1 modular squaring
9 modular additions/subtractions
```

The paper avoids treating every operation as a black box. Examples include:

- use a square directly in its following subtraction before uncomputing it;
- compute multiplication followed by addition without copying an intermediate product;
- clear inversion auxiliaries and reuse them for multiplication in modular division;
- correct the Kaliski pseudo-inverse in parallel with inversion uncomputation.

## Integrated ECDLP results

For a 256-bit curve, the low-width design reports:

```text
2124 logical qubits
```

versus 2338 in the prior RNSL estimate, with reported factors of 119 lower T count and 54 lower T depth.

## Windowing and representation

The paper uses signed windowing for scalar multiplication and sequential quantum lookup of classical points. Its modular multiplication is Montgomery based; inversion uses a fixed-round binary Kaliski procedure.

## Transfer boundary

The benchmark does not use the same scalar-multiplication wrapper or cost metric. Its current arithmetic is substantially newer. The paper remains valuable for whole-stack dependency structure, adaptive uncomputation, and as a control against which newer claims can be understood.

## Source limitations

- Counts are T-gate/T-depth/width estimates rather than benchmark executed-Toffoli products.
- The offset-point ABI differs from windowed scalar multiplication.
- The arithmetic predates newer pseudo-Mersenne and replay architectures.
- A complete return to this architecture is unlikely to beat the live benchmark.
