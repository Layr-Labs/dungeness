# Source: arXiv:2607.13816v2

## One-paragraph synthesis

**[PAPER]** Luo et al. give an exact, explicit, low-space reversible modular-inversion circuit and use it to build affine elliptic-curve point addition with three field-sized registers. The inversion is a four-phase extended Euclidean algorithm (EEA) whose decreasing remainders and increasing Bézout coefficients share two physical work registers. Four logarithmic-size length registers track the moving boundaries, and location-controlled arithmetic updates only the subranges that currently contain live values. A rigorous uniform schedule of at most `4 ceil(c n)` steps, where `c = 3/log2(2+sqrt(3))`, replaces an earlier inaccurate bound and makes the algorithm coherent over all inputs. Mid-circuit Hadamard-basis measurement, reset, recomputation, and classically controlled phase correction then recycle an obsolete field register inside in-place division and multiplication.

## Main claims

For an `n`-bit prime field:

```text
modular inversion qubits = 2n + 6 floor(log2 n) + 19
point-addition qubits     = 3n + 6 floor(log2 n) + 19
modular inversion Toffoli < 216.636 n^2 + O(n log n)
point-addition Toffoli    < 1003 n^2 + O(n log n)
full ECDLP Toffoli         = 1008 n^3/log2(n) + O(n^2)
```

At `n=256`:

| Circuit | Logical qubits | Toffolis | CNOTs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Modular inversion | 579 | 30.59 million | 43.39 million |
| Controlled affine point addition | 835 | 70.10 million | 108.09 million |
| Complete signed-window ECDLP estimate | 835 | approximately `2^30.88` | not reported as one total |

The concrete point-addition width follows exactly:

```text
3*256 + 6*floor(log2 256) + 19
= 768 + 48 + 19
= 835
```

## Fixed EEA schedule

The four-phase division/update schedule has input-dependent phase boundaries but an input-independent worst-case number of elementary steps:

```text
c = 3 / log2(2 + sqrt(3))
  = 1.5789734406...

T_max = 4 ceil(c n)
```

For `n=256`, the companion implementation uses:

```text
T_max = 1620
```

**[PAPER]** The appendix proves the bound by converting quotient sequences into weighted matrix products and certifying their minimum growth using a finite polyhedral antinorm. The asymptotically extremal pattern alternates quotient structure and grows at normalized rate `(2+sqrt(3))^(1/3)`. The paper explicitly corrects the earlier `4.5n` upper-bound claim attributed to Proos and Zalka.

## Register layout

**[PAPER]**

```text
Work1: n+3 qubits, storing t | q | r
Work2: n+3 qubits, storing t' and r'
Length: ell_t, ell_q, ell_r', ell_s
Control: Phase1, Phase2, Iter, Sign, Ctrl
```

Each length register has `floor(log2 n)+1` qubits. `Work2` is circularly shifted; during the middle phases, `t'` can be split between the two ends of the register. The key invariant supporting shared storage is:

```text
r_(i-1) * t_i + r_i * t_(i-1) = p
```

The forward inversion interface is:

```text
|x>|0^n>|0^S>
  -> |x^-1 mod p>|0^n>|Gamma(x)>
```

`Gamma(x)` is retained until the inverse EEA circuit clears it.

## Measurement-assisted register recycling

After a quotient or product has been computed into a clean accumulator `A`, the old `Y=y` is redundant but entangled with the rest of the computation. The paper:

1. applies `H^n` to `Y`;
2. measures outcome `b`;
3. resets `Y` to zero;
4. uses `Y` as the large workspace for inverse EEA;
5. recomputes `y` from the surviving values;
6. applies classically controlled `Z^b` to cancel phase `(-1)^(b dot y)`;
7. uncomputes the recomputation and swaps the output into `Y`.

This is stronger than deleting a one-bit predicate: it recycles a complete `n`-qubit value while preserving coherence through an explicit phase repair.

## Resource-count consistency note

**[PAPER]** The abstract, introduction table, and arithmetic implied by the full-ECDLP formula support `2^30.88` Toffolis for secp256k1:

```text
28 * (5*2^16 + 70.10 million)
≈ 1.944 billion
log2(...) ≈ 30.877
```

Section 6.4 contains one sentence saying `2^30.63`. That sentence is inconsistent with the displayed formula and the paper's other reported value. This KB uses `2^30.88` and records `2^30.63` as a source typo, not as a second estimate.

## Relationship to the earlier KB paper

The two papers optimize different points:

- Schrottenloher, arXiv:2606.02235, uses a compact branch-history/dialog architecture and achieves much smaller gate counts at roughly 1192 qubits.
- Luo et al., arXiv:2607.13816v2, keeps explicit EEA state in two shared work registers and reaches 835 qubits, but the point-addition and full-ECDLP gate counts are much larger.

**[INFERENCE]** For ecdsa.fail, the most promising use is therefore selective: import register-lifetime reasoning and measured field-register recycling into a lower-gate architecture before considering a full four-phase EEA port.

## Source limitations

- The paper's top-level controlled addend is selected from precomputed classical points; ecdsa.fail receives runtime classical coordinate bits.
- The paper optimizes logical qubits, not the benchmark product `average executed Toffoli * peak qubits`.
- Mid-circuit measurement circuits need benchmark-specific forward/reverse and transcript handling; a dynamic circuit cannot be inverted by blindly reversing its gates.
- Numerical resource counts are generated blockwise from Qiskit definitions, not by flattening and executing the complete secp256k1 circuit as one state-vector simulation.
- Companion-repository test claims are source-reported validation unless independently rerun.
