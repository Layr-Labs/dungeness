# Source: arXiv:2110.08973v3

## One-paragraph synthesis

**[PAPER]** The spooky pebble game models quantum workspace recycling by Hadamard-basis measurement, classical storage of outcomes, and later adaptive phase correction. The paper gives tight line-graph time–qubit tradeoffs for any pebble bound, derives general sequential-computation simulations, proves hardness for arbitrary DAG optimization, and gives an optimal-pebble efficient strategy for complete binary trees.

## Line frontier

If:

```text
n <= binomial(m+s-2, s-2) + 1
```

then a line of length `n` can be spooky-pebbled with `s` pebbles in `O(mn)` steps, with a matching asymptotic lower bound.

Representative regimes:

```text
3 pebbles          -> O(n^2) steps
O(log n) pebbles   -> O(n log n) steps
O(n^epsilon)       -> O(n/epsilon) steps
```

For a complete binary tree with `n = 2^h - 1`, the paper gives `h+1` pebbles and `O(n log n)` steps.

## Meaning of a ghost

A measured checkpoint is not simply erased. Its classical measurement result records a possible phase obligation. To “unghost” it, the computation must later reproduce enough dependency state to apply the corresponding phase correction.

## Transfer boundary

The current tape/replay schedule is a specialized line-like dependency problem, but each checkpoint stores wide `(u,v)` state and replay has asymmetric costs. The theorem is a design lens, not a promise that a generic schedule wins.

## Source limitations

- Classical bits grow as qubits shrink; total memory does not necessarily decrease.
- The abstract step cost does not capture the very different Toffoli costs of walk, replay, fold, and comparison cells.
- Finding an optimal spooky strategy for a general DAG is PSPACE-hard to approximate.
- Local generic checkpointing has already been measured unfavorable for the current ping-pong circuit.
