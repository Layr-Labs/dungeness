# Paper 2607.13816 — Introduction and contributions

Luo et al. ask how far the logical-qubit requirement of Shor's elliptic-curve discrete-logarithm algorithm can be reduced while retaining an explicit, exact reversible circuit. Their target is the main space bottleneck in affine-coordinate point addition: modular inversion.

## Position in the design space

**[PAPER]** Previous work supplies several distinct optimization points:

- low gate count with more than one thousand logical qubits;
- low depth with substantial ancillary space;
- very low space using residue-number-system or compressed-output techniques, at extremely high gate cost;
- the Proos–Zalka register-sharing idea, which had not been developed into a fully explicit exact circuit with detailed ancilla management.

This paper develops the last route. It supersedes arXiv:2604.02311 and improves its own earlier space formulas from roughly `5n` to `3n` for point addition and from `3n` to `2n` for modular inversion.

## Contributions

### 1. Exact shared-register EEA

The paper stores decreasing EEA remainders and increasing Bézout coefficients in two overlapping `n+3`-qubit work registers. Four length registers track the live boundaries, allowing a deterministic allocation for every superposed input rather than discarding rare large states.

See:

- [`luo-shared-work-registers.md`](../nodes/luo-shared-work-registers.md)
- [`luo-length-registers.md`](../nodes/luo-length-registers.md)

### 2. Rigorous fixed schedule

The phase progression depends on the input, but the same elementary step circuit can be repeated a fixed maximum number of times:

```text
T_max = 4 ceil(c n)
c = 3/log2(2+sqrt(3)) ≈ 1.5789734406
```

The bound is exact for all valid inputs and asymptotically sharp in its constant. This is crucial for applying the circuit coherently to a superposition.

See [`luo-uniform-step-bound.md`](../nodes/luo-uniform-step-bound.md).

### 3. Explicit location-controlled arithmetic

The physical positions occupied by `t`, `q`, `r`, `t'`, and `r'` vary coherently. Arithmetic must therefore be selected by quantum length registers. The paper implements location-controlled swap, add/subtract, and length update using unary iteration and step-dependent active windows.

See:

- [`luo-active-windows.md`](../nodes/luo-active-windows.md)
- [`luo-unary-iteration.md`](../nodes/luo-unary-iteration.md)
- [`luo-location-controlled-add-sub.md`](../nodes/luo-location-controlled-add-sub.md)

### 4. Full-register measurement recycling

The authors free an obsolete `n`-qubit field register by measuring it in the Hadamard basis. They preserve coherence by tracking the induced phase, recomputing the old value later, applying classically controlled `Z` corrections, and uncomputing. This lets in-place division and multiplication use only three field registers.

See [`luo-hadamard-register-recycling.md`](../nodes/luo-hadamard-register-recycling.md).

### 5. Concrete resources

For secp256k1-sized fields, the paper reports:

```text
modular inversion: 579 qubits, 30.59M Toffolis
point addition:    835 qubits, 70.10M Toffolis
full ECDLP:        835 qubits, about 2^30.88 Toffolis
```

The result is a new low-space point, not a simultaneous gate-count improvement. See [`luo-resource-estimates.md`](../nodes/luo-resource-estimates.md).

## Implementation significance

The construction establishes an exact low-space point rather than a simultaneous minimum in space and gates.

**[INFERENCE]** Its mechanisms can be evaluated at three levels:

- adopt full-register measurement recycling while retaining an existing arithmetic core;
- use active-window and dirty-borrowing proofs to reduce peak workspace;
- port the complete four-phase shared-register EEA when minimizing logical width justifies its gate cost.

See [`luo-resource-tradeoffs.md`](../concepts/luo-resource-tradeoffs.md).
